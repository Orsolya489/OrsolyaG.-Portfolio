Built to calm the fear. Realised the data was the threat, not the answer.

When people are worried about losing their jobs, showing them a screen packed with numbers and alerts doesn't inform them. It confirms the fear. Mindure started as an attempt to fix the interface. It ended as an argument about what the interface is actually for.

01 — The moment it became necessary

The problem wasn't that people lacked data.

Career tools in 2024 were full of data. The problem was timing — information arriving at exactly the moment a person was least able to receive it.

02 — The lens

Automation anxiety isn't one thing.
It's three domains colliding at once.

Anxiety narrows attention
The brain processes less under threat, not more.
Cognitive load has a hard ceiling
Exceed it and the brain shuts down — not slows down.
Automation anxiety erodes autonomy
It erodes the ability to make your own decisions.
Read the research →

Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive load theory — working memory has a strict capacity limit. When exceeded, processing stops entirely.

Easterbrook, J.A. (1959). Threat signals narrow attention to immediate danger, away from strategic thinking.

Bhorat, Z. (2023). Automation Anxiety and a Right to Freedom from Automated Systems and AI. Carr Center for Human Rights Policy, Harvard Kennedy School. Automated systems that score and decide on behalf of users threaten three core human abilities: to work, to understand information, and to make autonomous decisions. carrcenter.hks.harvard.edu

03 — The system

A conceptual multi-source model.
The design constraint was that the system could only surface what the person was ready to act on.

Macroeconomic signals, skills gap mapping, and stress-state detection feeding a single output layer. Everything else had to wait.

Every section also tells users where its data comes from — in plain language, at the moment they need it. Not in a privacy policy. Not in a help article. Right there.

04 — AI output vs. human redesign

The first version was AI-generated.
It was comprehensive, technically coherent, and cognitively harmful.

This section is a side-by-side account of what it got wrong — and why each redesign decision traces back to a human state, not a design preference.

Overview
Ten scores at once. The brain doesn't know where to start — so it stops.
HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP
RAW AI OUTPUT
❌ AI Output

10 scored modules displayed simultaneously. No visual hierarchy, no entry point.

Without a single place to start, the brain cannot begin — so it freezes instead.

The cognitive science →
Sweller, J. (1988) — exceeding working memory capacity triggers shutdown, not slower processing. The brain does not gracefully degrade; it stops.
✓ Human Redesign

Five cards. One thing to look at first.

One entry point reduces decision paralysis. Volatile data is hidden until the user is ready.

The HF/HAI principle →
Progressive disclosure — one entry point, volatile data hidden until requested. Designed to match the actual capacity of an anxious brain on first contact.
Personal Insights
Putting a number on how someone thinks turns self-knowledge into a test score.
HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP
RAW AI OUTPUT
❌ AI Output

Percentage scores on cognitive traits: 94% spatial reasoning, 87% pattern recognition.

Automated scores on internal states threaten the person's ability to trust their own self-understanding.

The cognitive science →
Bhorat, Z. (2023) — scoring internal states with automated systems threatens decision-making autonomy. Carr Center, Harvard Kennedy School. Knowing a machine has scored your mind erodes confidence in your own judgment.
✓ Human Redesign

"How your mind works" — strengths in plain language, compared only to your own history.

Self-referential framing removes competitive threat. Insight compared to your own baseline, not population averages.

The HF/HAI principle →
Self-referential framing — all patterns compared to the user's own history, never external benchmarks. Removes the performance threat embedded in percentile scoring.
Transparency layer
The system tells you where each conclusion came from — before you have to trust it.
Every insight shows its source in plain language, at the moment you're reading it. Not buried in a settings page.
The HF/HAI principle →
Explainability at the point of inference — transparency about data source reduces perceived threat of automated judgment. Related to EU AI Act Article 13 (transparency obligations) and Bhorat (2023) — knowing the source of automated content is a core human entitlement.
Career Actions
Telling someone exactly what to do and when removes the one thing that reduces anxiety: feeling in control.
HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP
RAW AI OUTPUT
❌ AI Output

Actionable roadmap with exact task times, effort labels, deadlines, and a cognitive load tip telling you when to work.

Prescribed commands remove perceived control — the primary mediator of anxiety.

The cognitive science →
Rotter, J.B. (1966) — perceived control is the primary mediator of anxiety response. Remove it and anxiety rises. When a system commands rather than offers, it takes the one thing that calms a frightened person away from them.
✓ Human Redesign

Live job postings with visible reasoning — "why it fits your thinking style" — and an ethical boundary persistent in the sidebar.

The system surfaces options and reasoning. The user decides what to do with them.

The HF/HAI principle →
Autonomy-preserving design — the system offers options with visible reasoning, never commands actions. The human remains the decision-maker at every step.
Market & Economy
Live market data updating every 2 minutes doesn't inform a scared person. It keeps them scared.
HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP
RAW AI OUTPUT
❌ AI Output

Live economic indicators — GDP, unemployment, inflation, tech sector — all updating in real time, timestamped "2 min ago."

Live tickers are threat signals. Threat signals redirect attention from strategic thinking to immediate danger scanning.

The cognitive science →
Easterbrook, J.A. (1959) — threat signals redirect attention from strategic processing to immediate danger scanning. A live updating number is a threat signal, even when the number is neutral.
✓ Human Redesign

Market Intelligence — the same data framed as background context for decisions, not a live urgency feed.

Volatile data framed as stable context. The user chooses when to engage with it, rather than being triggered.

The HF/HAI principle →
Context before signal — volatile data is real but framed as stable background. The user chooses when to engage with it, rather than being triggered by it continuously.
Skills Builder
A rigid learning list shows someone what they're missing. A connected path shows where they're going.
HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP
RAW AI OUTPUT
❌ AI Output

Linear learning path: Fundamentals → 3D Modeling → CAD → Architecture → VR Design → Parametric → AI Integration. Fixed sequence, no reasoning visible.

Imposed sequences undermine both competence and autonomy — the two conditions required for motivated learning.

The cognitive science →
Deci, E.L. & Ryan, R.M. (1985) — competence and autonomy are prerequisite conditions for motivated learning. Imposed sequences undermine both, replacing intrinsic motivation with compliance.
✓ Human Redesign

Smart Skills Builder — learning tied to cognitive profile and career direction. Reasoning visible. Progress shown relative to your own goals.

Learning tied to who you are, not an externally defined sequence. Progress is self-referential, not ranked.

The HF/HAI principle →
Connected path design — courses surface with visible reasoning tied to the user's own profile. Progress is measured against personal goals, never external benchmarks.
Transparency layer
Recommendations show their working. You decide whether you agree.
Course suggestions explain their reasoning at the moment they appear — so the user stays in control of the decision.
The HF/HAI principle →
Autonomy-preserving transparency — surfacing reasoning at the point of recommendation keeps the human as the decision-maker. Consistent with self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985) — autonomy requires understanding the basis of choices presented to you.
Strategic Planning
Scheduling someone's life down to the hour is not support. It's the illusion of certainty.
HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP
RAW AI OUTPUT
❌ AI Output

Daily calendar with exact time blocks. The algorithm commands the exact schedule hour by hour.

Rigid plans increase anxiety when disrupted. Certainty that breaks is worse than no certainty at all.

The cognitive science →
Uncertainty tolerance research — rigid plans increase anxiety when disrupted. The more precise the plan, the more painful the deviation. Flexible frameworks reduce anticipatory stress by removing the expectation of exact compliance.
✓ Human Redesign

2026 Activity Roadmap — quarterly blocks, flexible framing, stress regulation practice alongside courses.

Quarterly direction provides orientation without over-specifying the path. Long-term clarity without short-term pressure.

The HF/HAI principle →
Temporal breathing room — quarterly direction replaces daily commands. Long-term clarity paired with short-term flexibility reduces anticipatory anxiety while maintaining motivation and direction.
05 — What changed

No live users, no A/B test.
What the project produced instead is a documented position.

Comprehensiveness and clarity are in direct tension for someone already overwhelmed. That tension doesn't resolve with better UI. It resolves with a different understanding of what the system is for.

06 — What I'd do differently

I would start with stress-state research before touching the information architecture.

I designed the calm state first and worked backwards. That was the wrong sequence — the anxiety state should have been the primary design surface, not the edge case.

"A system that gives you everything it knows is not a helpful system. Helpfulness is knowing what to withhold, and when."
